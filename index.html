C3.1.17HL ONLY

Tropic responses in seedlings

A tropism is directional growth towards or away from an external stimulus. Seedling coleoptiles or hypocotyls curve as cells on opposite sides elongate differently.

Record qualitative observations as sketches or descriptions; record quantitative observations as curvature angles or growth rates. Manual protractors introduce parallax and alignment error. Standardise the camera and light, repeat trials, and use image analysis to improve measurement quality.

Keep the terms distinct: replication supports reliability; fine-scale digital measurement can improve precision and accuracy.

MCQ 1Choose one

Which is a quantitative record of a seedling’s tropic response?

Select one answer
Reveal answer

Answer: B. An angle in degrees is a numerical measurement.

MCQ 2Choose one

Which change most directly improves reliability?

Select one answer
Reveal answer

Answer: A. Repeated trials show variation and help identify unusual results.

C3.1.18HL ONLY

Positive phototropism in shoots

Positive phototropism is shoot growth towards lateral light. Phototropins detect blue light; the signal redistributes auxin towards the shaded side. In shoots, higher auxin there promotes cell elongation, so that side grows faster and the shoot curves towards light. It is differential expansion, not faster cell division.

Classic evidence: Darwin’s coleoptile tests localised light detection to the tip. Boysen-Jensen and Went supported the idea of a mobile chemical signal.

MCQ 1Choose one

Which sequence best explains a shoot bending towards one-sided blue light?

Select one answer
Reveal answer

Answer: A. Unequal elongation on the shaded side produces curvature toward light.

MCQ 2Choose one

What did the coleoptile-tip experiments support?

Select one answer
Reveal answer

Answer: A. Tip-covering and barrier experiments showed that the tip senses light and a chemical signal can move to the elongation region.

Chemical signals

Signal and transport

C3.1.19HL ONLY

Phytohormones coordinate plant responses

Phytohormones are chemical signals active at very low concentrations. They regulate growth, development and responses to stimuli. Plants use several types, including auxins, cytokinins, gibberellins and abscisic acid; they can be made in many tissues rather than in dedicated endocrine glands.

Keep receptors distinct from hormones: phytochrome detects red and far-red light. Its Pr and Pfr forms help plants respond to night length, shade and flowering cues.

MCQ 1Choose one

Which statement best describes phytohormones?

Select one answer
Reveal answer

Answer: A. Phytohormones are chemical messengers used across plant tissues.

MCQ 2Choose one

Which molecule is a light receptor rather than a phytohormone?

Select one answer
Reveal answer

Answer: C. Phytochrome is a photoreceptor protein; it detects red and far-red light.

C3.1.20HL ONLY

PIN carriers maintain auxin gradients

Auxin (IAA) can enter cells from the acidic apoplast. At about pH 5.5, some IAA is protonated and uncharged, so it crosses the membrane. In the near-neutral cytoplasm, it loses a proton and becomes charged IAA⁻, which cannot diffuse back through the lipid membrane.

PIN efflux carriers move auxin out. Their asymmetric position in cell membranes makes transport polar: basal PIN placement supports movement from shoot tips toward roots, while lateral repositioning can redirect auxin across a shoot.

MCQ 1Choose one

Why can auxin enter a plant cell more freely than it leaves?

Select one answer
Reveal answer

Answer: A. Protonated IAA can cross the membrane; ionised IAA⁻ needs an efflux carrier to exit.

MCQ 2Choose one

What makes auxin transport polar through a row of cells?

Select one answer
Reveal answer

Answer: A. Asymmetric PIN location directs auxin efflux to one side of each cell.

Differential growth

Loosen, extend, coordinate

C3.1.21HL ONLY

Auxin promotes cell elongation

In a shoot elongation zone, auxin stimulates proton pumps in the plasma membrane. H⁺ enters the apoplast, lowering cell-wall pH. Expansins then loosen links in the wall; they do not hydrolyse covalent bonds.

Water uptake raises turgor pressure, which extends the softened wall and elongates the cell. Unequal auxin concentrations therefore create unequal elongation rates, such as the bending in phototropism.

MCQ 1Choose one

What is the immediate effect of auxin-stimulated proton pumps?

Select one answer
Reveal answer

Answer: A. Proton secretion acidifies the apoplast and activates wall-loosening processes.

MCQ 2Choose one

Once the wall is loosened, what helps extend the cell?

Select one answer
Reveal answer

Answer: A. Turgor pressure pushes against the yielding wall and drives cell elongation.

C3.1.22HL ONLY

Auxin and cytokinin integrate root and shoot growth

Shoot tips and young leaves produce auxin, which travels down towards roots. Root tips produce cytokinin, which travels up to shoots. This two-way signalling coordinates growth above and below ground.

In tissue culture, a high auxin-to-cytokinin ratio favours roots; a low ratio favours shoot buds; similar levels can maintain callus growth. In an intact shoot, auxin from the terminal bud suppresses axillary buds while cytokinin promotes their growth.

MCQ 1Choose one

Which pair of transport directions is correct?

Select one answer
Reveal answer

Answer: A. Auxin moves down from shoot tips; cytokinin moves up from root tips.

MCQ 2Choose one

What does a high auxin-to-cytokinin ratio usually promote in tissue culture?

Select one answer
Reveal answer

Answer: A. A relatively high auxin level promotes root development.

D

Feedback and timing

Synchronise ripening

C3.1.23HL ONLY

Ethylene creates positive feedback during fruit ripening

Ethylene (ethene) is a volatile gaseous phytohormone. It promotes fruit-ripening changes such as starch-to-sugar conversion, softening, loss of organic acids and aroma production. Ripening tissue releases more ethylene, which accelerates further ripening: a positive-feedback loop.

Why it helps: ethylene diffuses through a fruit cluster, promoting rapid, synchronised ripening and making fruit conspicuous to seed-dispersing animals.

MCQ 1Choose one

Which sequence describes positive feedback in fruit ripening?

Select one answer
Reveal answer

Answer: A. Ripening increases ethylene production, and the extra ethylene accelerates ripening.

MCQ 2Choose one

What is a likely benefit of synchronised fruit ripening?

Select one answer
Reveal answer

Answer: A. A ripe cluster is more conspicuous and can attract frugivores that disperse seeds.

05 · Bring it together

Plenary: explain the whole chain

Return to the seedling from the starter. Use the terms from the lesson to connect a stimulus to an integrated plant response.

How does unilateral light produce a curved shoot?

Model answer: Phototropin detects blue light; PIN carriers redistribute auxin towards the shaded flank; auxin promotes wall loosening there; cells elongate faster on that side, bending the shoot toward light.

How are root and shoot growth coordinated?

Model answer: Auxin moves from shoot tips towards roots, while cytokinin moves from root tips towards shoots. Their interaction balances growth of both systems.

Why is ethylene ripening positive feedback rather than negative feedback?

Model answer: Ripening increases ethylene production, and the additional ethylene stimulates more ripening, amplifying the change.
